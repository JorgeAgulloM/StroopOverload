# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Competitive, self-improving individuals aged 18–35. Gamers and performance-oriented people who enjoy reaction challenges and measurable cognitive metrics. They arrive wanting to be tested — not hand-held.

## Product Purpose

A Stroop effect-based cognitive training game that forces the brain to override automatic word-reading in favor of color identification. The game cranks up speed, pressure, and complexity — turning a classic psychology experiment into a brutal skill test. Success = players coming back to beat their own time, brag their scores, and improve cognitive speed under pressure.

## Brand Personality

Aggressive. Electric. Sharp.

Voice: Direct, confrontational, no fluff. The brand challenges — it doesn't encourage. Think: your fastest opponent, not your coach.

Emotional goal: Adrenaline at the start screen. Obsession with the next run.

## Anti-references

- Lumosity and all pastel, bubbly brain-training apps — rounded cards, cheerful color blobs, nurturing copy
- Generic SaaS landing pages — hero + 3 feature cards + pricing table formula
- Clinical / sterile lab aesthetics — psychology textbook, experiment report, white space as default
- Childish gamification — badges, stars, reward confetti, leaderboard ribbons as primary UI motivation

## Design Principles

1. **Tension by design** — the interface itself should feel like a challenge. No comfort zones, no soft landings. The UI is already testing you before you click play.
2. **Arcade legacy, modern execution** — draw energy from retro game aesthetics (scanlines, neon, score counters) without nostalgia-bait or literal pixel art kitsch. Reference the feeling, not the frame.
3. **Speed is the product** — fast transitions, instant feedback, no loading ceremony. Lag kills immersion; every interaction should feel sub-100ms snappy.
4. **Earn every element** — nothing decorative that can't justify its cognitive load. Each visual decision either amplifies intensity or gets cut.
5. **Performance is identity** — the game is about measurable output. Design should reflect that: numbers, precision, score, time. Data is decoration here.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Critical consideration: the Stroop task is fundamentally about color perception — colorblind users experience a different (sometimes absent) conflict. The game must provide color-independent alternatives or explicitly communicate this limitation. Reduced-motion alternative for any animated transitions.
